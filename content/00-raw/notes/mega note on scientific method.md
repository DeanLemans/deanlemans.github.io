## Before start

- a clear research question (specific and measurable, not vaag)
- a hypothesis (what you expect to find)
- background reading (what people already know, where the gap is)

## The study itself

- study design (experiment, survey, observation, case study, can be all/can be 1?)
- variables (what you change, what you measure, what you keep the same)
- sample (who or what, how many, how you picked them)
- control group or baseline (so you have smth to compare against)
- randomization (if it fits the study)
- blinding (only if relevant)
- procedure (exact steps so someone else can repeat it)
- materials and tools (what you used)
- data collection (what you measured, how, when, units)
- data analysis (how you processed the data, which tests)
- ethics (consent, privacy, personal data)

## Writing it

- title: short, descriptive, includes topic and variables
	- (if relevant, subtitle)
- authors and affiliations
- abstract: background, aim, methods, key results, conclusion (150-250 words)
- keywords (optional)
- introduction: context, gap in knowledge, research question and hypothesis
- methods: design, participants or materials, procedure, data analysis, ethics
- results: findings only, tables and figures, no interpretation yet
- discussion: answer the question, compare with literature, limitations, implications, future research
- conclusion: short answer to the research question
- references: consistent style (APA, Vancouver, etc.), all cited sources
- formatting: clear headings, numbered sections, consistent font and spacing, page numbers
- tables and figures: numbered, captioned, referred to in text
- appendices (if needed): raw data, extra materials

## What makes it scientific

- repeatable (someone else can do it again)
- transparent (data, method, and analysis are visible)
- honest about limits (small sample, bias, errors)
- checked by others (peer review)
